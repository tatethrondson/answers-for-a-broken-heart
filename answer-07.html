Answer 07 · Why Did This Happen?

Can anything good actually come out of this?

Romans 8:28 does not say everything that happens is good. It says God is able to work even what is broken into a story of redemption.

About a 9 minute read · Biblical · Pastoral · Honest

The short answer

All things—even this.

Yes. God can bring good through what He never asks you to call good. Scripture does not minimize evil or loss; it promises that neither is beyond His ability to redeem. Sometimes that good becomes visible quickly. Sometimes it takes years. Sometimes we will not see the full picture until eternity.

Romans 8:28 does not call everything good.

This verse is often quoted to hurting people so quickly that it can feel like a way of rushing past their pain.

Romans 8:28 · KJV
“And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.”

Notice what Paul does not say. He does not say all things are good. Cancer is not good. Betrayal is not good. Abuse is not good. A funeral is not good simply because God is present there.

Paul says God can make all things work together for good. The promise is not about the goodness of the wound. It is about the power of the Redeemer.

God does not have to call the wound good in order to bring good through it.

What does “good” mean?

The next verse helps define it. Romans 8:29 speaks about believers being conformed to the image of Christ. Sometimes the good God is producing is not the restoration of the life we had before. Sometimes it is a deeper Christlikeness, a compassion we could not have learned from a distance, a ministry to wounded people, or a dependence on God we never would have chosen.

That does not mean pain is the only way God can grow us. It means pain is not wasted when it arrives.

Joseph understood that after years of betrayal, slavery, false accusation, and imprisonment. He could look at the evil his brothers intended and still see a larger providence at work.

Genesis 50:20 · KJV
“But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive.”

Joseph did not rename evil as good. He said God was able to write a better purpose through what others meant for harm.

The crack does not have to be hidden.

There is an old Japanese art called kintsugi in which broken pottery is repaired so the seams remain visible, often highlighted rather than concealed. The repaired piece does not pretend it was never broken.

That picture has stayed with me because redemption is not the same as erasure. God does not always restore your life by making it look exactly as it did before. Sometimes His grace becomes most visible along the very line where life broke.

If you are still standing among the pieces, you may not be able to see that yet. You do not have to. Romans 8:28 was not written to demand that you admire the pain while you are still bleeding from it. It was written to assure you that the story is not outside God’s hands.

Sometimes you get to see the arc later.

Our son Caden has lived his whole life with the effects of a severe birth injury to his arm. There were surgeries, therapy, limitations, and questions we could not answer when he was little.

Years later, when Debbie expressed the kind of guilt mothers sometimes carry and told him she wished she could take those hardships away, Caden said something that stopped us: he would not change it if he could, because he had seen how God used it to shape who he became.

He was not saying the injury was good. He was saying God had been good through it.

Sometimes the evidence of Romans 8:28 only becomes visible in the rearview mirror.

If you cannot see the good yet

Please do not force it. A verse can be true before you are emotionally ready to see how. You are allowed to grieve the wound while trusting that God’s work is not finished.

What do I do with this tonight?

Do not ask yourself to find five positive things about the worst thing that happened to you. That is not the promise. Instead ask a smaller question: “God, what part of me are You still able to reach here?” Maybe it is your compassion. Maybe your dependence. Maybe a future ministry you cannot imagine yet. Maybe tonight the only good you can identify is that you are still talking to Him.

Leave room for God to write chapters you cannot read yet.

So here is Answer #7, stated plainly: yes, God can bring real good out of what is genuinely painful and wrong. He does not waste wounds, and He is capable of writing redemption through chapters you would never have chosen.
