## Answer 01

**Question:** Has God really been here the whole time, or have I just told myself that to feel better?

**Short answer:** He’s always been there.

Scripture consistently shows a God who moves toward people before they know how to move toward Him. Pain can make His presence difficult to recognize, but difficulty recognizing Him is not the same thing as His absence.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · Psalm 34:18

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

**Existing prayer:** “Lord, I cannot feel You clearly right now. Help me trust Your promise more than my perception.”

**Existing next step:** Tell God one honest sentence about where He feels absent, then read Psalm 34:18 slowly twice.

## Answer 05

**Question:** Is it wrong that I keep asking God why?

**Short answer:** Honest questions are not unbelief.

No. Asking God why does not automatically mean you have stopped trusting Him. Job asked. David asked. John the Baptist asked. God welcomes the honest cry of a hurting heart that still turns toward Him—even when the answer does not come quickly.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · Psalm 13:1

“How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me?”

**Existing prayer:** “God, I have questions I cannot silence. Help me bring them to You instead of carrying them alone.”

**Existing next step:** Write your hardest why-question exactly as you would say it to God. Do not soften it.

## Answer 09

**Question:** Does God actually know what this feels like?

**Short answer:** He wept with you.

Jesus did not merely study suffering from heaven. He entered a human body, lived in a broken world, stood beside graves, felt betrayal and rejection, and wept with people He loved. He knows grief from the inside. You are not bringing your pain to a God who has never felt its weight.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · John 11:35

“Jesus wept.”

**Existing prayer:** “Jesus, thank You for entering human sorrow. Sit with me in mine.”

**Existing next step:** Picture Christ at Lazarus's grave. Let yourself grieve without apologizing for it.

## Answer 13

**Question:** What do I do when God says no?

**Short answer:** A no is not the end of the story.

God’s refusal of a specific request is not proof that He has stopped loving you or stopped working. Jesus heard “no” in Gethsemane. Paul heard “no” about his thorn. In both cases, God was doing something larger than the immediate relief they asked for. A no can be a chapter without being the ending.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · 2 Corinthians 12:9

“My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.”

**Existing prayer:** “Lord, I wanted a different answer. Meet me in the disappointment and give me grace for what is.”

**Existing next step:** Let yourself name what you hoped God would do. Grieve that honestly before trying to find a lesson.

## Answer 14

**Question:** Is this really the end?

**Short answer:** Death does not get the final word.

Christian hope rests on one historical claim: Jesus Christ rose from the dead. Because He lives, death is still an enemy but no longer an undefeated one. For the believer, grief is real, separation is painful, and the grave is temporary. Resurrection means the story continues beyond what we can presently see.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · John 11:25

“I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live.”

**Existing prayer:** “Jesus, the separation hurts. Hold my grief inside the hope of Your resurrection.”

**Existing next step:** Say the person's name today and thank God for one specific memory. Grief does not require forgetting.

## Answer 15

**Question:** How long am I allowed to still be sad about this?

**Short answer:** You’re allowed to grieve as long as it takes.

The Bible does not command you to hurry through grief. It gives grieving people language, room, tears, lament, community, and hope. Healing can be slow, uneven, and deeply personal. Still hurting does not mean you are failing God.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · Psalm 56:8

“Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle...”

**Existing prayer:** “God, receive the grief I am still carrying. Keep me from shaming myself for missing someone I loved.”

**Existing next step:** Give yourself permission to be sad today without measuring whether you should be over it by now.

## Answer 18

**Question:** Am I allowed to be furious with God?

**Short answer:** You are allowed to tell God you are angry.

That does not make every angry thought right, but anger is not automatic proof that faith is gone. Scripture contains prayers, laments, accusations, and cries from people who were deeply angry with God. Honest anger brought to Him is not the same as rejecting Him. God may correct what is wrong in us, but He does not require fake calm before we come. In many cases, the fact that you are still arguing with God means you still believe He is there and that what happened matters to Him.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · Psalm 62:8

“Pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us.”

**Existing prayer:** “God, I am angry. I do not want anger to drive me away from You, so I am bringing it to You.”

**Existing next step:** Pray the sentence you have been censoring. God already knows it; honesty keeps the conversation open.

## Answer 19

**Question:** What do I even say to God right now?

**Short answer:** Bring Him the real prayer, not the polished one.

God already knows what you feel. Prayer is not the place to hide it. Bring Him the anger, accusation, confusion, fear, disappointment, and grief as they actually are. Honest prayer keeps pain moving toward God instead of letting it harden inside you.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · Romans 8:26

“The Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought...”

**Existing prayer:** “God, I do not even know what to say. Hear what I cannot put into words.”

**Existing next step:** Set a timer for two minutes and talk to God without editing yourself. Silence counts too.

## Answer 21

**Question:** How am I supposed to forgive someone who never even said sorry?

**Short answer:** Forgiveness releases vengeance without calling the wrong right.

Forgiveness does not require the other person’s apology before you can begin. It is an act before God in which you release personal vengeance, refuse to keep collecting the debt in your own heart, and trust God with justice. It does not mean the wrong was acceptable, harmless, or forgotten.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · Ephesians 4:32

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you.”

**Existing prayer:** “Father, I do not want what they did to keep shaping me. Help me release vengeance without denying the truth.”

**Existing next step:** Do not force a feeling of forgiveness today. Begin by telling God exactly what you are releasing to His justice.

---

## Answer 22

**Question:** Does forgiving them mean I have to let them back in?

**Short answer:** Forgiveness is not reconciliation.

Forgiveness releases personal vengeance before God. Reconciliation rebuilds relationship. Forgiveness can happen with one willing heart; reconciliation requires truth, repentance, safety, and rebuilt trust from both sides. You can forgive completely and still maintain wise distance.

**Featured Scripture:** One Scripture · Matthew 3:8

“Bring forth therefore fruits meet for repentance.”

**Existing prayer:** “God, give me grace to forgive and wisdom about trust, access, and boundaries.”

**Existing next step:** Do not make a major reconciliation decision while pressured. Ask: Has repentance produced trustworthy fruit?
