Answer 23 · Doubt & Church Hurt

Am I walking away from God, or from something else wearing His name?

Before you decide what to do with faith, separate Jesus from the hypocrisy, abuse, legalism, or disappointment people may have attached to His name.

About a 9 minute read · Biblical · Pastoral · For doubters
Written by Tate Throndson · Senior pastor of Castleview Baptist Church in Castle Rock, Colorado · Pastor and author of Answers for a Broken Heart
Explore this topic: Doubt, Church Hurt & Faith →

The short answer

Separate Jesus from what was done in His name.

Sometimes people are not walking away from Jesus at all. They are walking away from hypocrisy, abuse, legalism, prosperity promises, manipulation, or a distorted picture of God that Scripture never taught. Rejecting a counterfeit can be part of finding the real Christ.

If you only have 60 seconds

One truth · One Scripture · One prayer · One step
One truth

Before rejecting Jesus, make sure you are not rejecting a distortion of Him.

One Scripture · John 6:68

“Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life.”

Pray this

“Jesus, help me separate You from the hypocrisy, abuse, legalism, or disappointment I have associated with Your name.”

One next step

Write down what you are actually questioning: Jesus Himself, or something people did while using His name.

If your pain has become dangerous or you are afraid you may hurt yourself, do not carry that moment alone. Use the immediate safety pathway →

If you are standing somewhere near the doorway of faith, I do not want to pressure you back inside with fear.

I want to ask a more careful question.

What exactly are you leaving?

Because “Christianity” can mean very different things depending on what someone experienced under that name.

Do not let someone else’s distortion of God make your final decision about God.

Some versions of God deserve to be rejected.

If you were taught that enough faith guarantees healing, Scripture does not promise that.

If you were taught that faithful Christians will be protected from tragedy, Scripture does not promise that.

If you were taught that unanswered prayer always means hidden sin or weak faith, Scripture does not say that.

If you were taught that a church leader’s authority means they cannot be questioned, corrected, reported, or held accountable, that is not biblical Christianity.

If you were hurt by cruelty wrapped in Christian vocabulary, calling the cruelty wrong is not rebellion against Christ.

Look at Jesus without the wrapping.

Strip away the slogans, personalities, disappointments, church politics, and things people promised in God’s name.

Then look at Jesus Himself.

He touched people everyone else avoided.

He wept at a grave.

He confronted religious hypocrisy with some of the strongest language He ever used.

He welcomed children. He defended the vulnerable. He ate with people respectable religion kept at a distance.

And He gave His life for sinners.

John 1:14 · KJV
“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us… full of grace and truth.”

Grace and truth.

Not manipulation and denial.

Not comfort promises God never made.

Not pretending sin is harmless.

Grace and truth together.

The real Jesus never promised a painless life.

John 16:33 · KJV
“In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

That is very different from a faith that says obedience will keep suffering away.

Jesus tells the truth about the world.

You will have trouble.

And then He gives a different promise: not immunity from trouble, but Himself in the middle of it and ultimate victory beyond it.

A faith built on “nothing bad will happen” collapses when something bad happens. Jesus never asked you to build there.

Church hurt and Jesus are not identical.

The church matters deeply. Jesus loves His church.

And churches can fail people.

Pastors can say foolish things. Leaders can sin. Christians can be hypocritical, dismissive, controlling, or cruel. Sometimes what happened was not merely “church hurt” in a vague sense—it was abuse or serious wrongdoing.

Naming that accurately does not require rejecting Christ.

If someone used God’s name to hurt you

You do not have to defend what happened in order to explore faith honestly. Wrong is wrong even when a Bible verse was attached to it. You may need distance from a particular person, church, or environment while you sort through what is true. Take the time you need to separate Jesus from the ways people misrepresented Him.

Test what you were taught.

Scripture does not ask for mindless belief.

Acts 17:11 · KJV
“They received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.”

The Bereans were commended for checking teaching against Scripture.

You are allowed to ask, Is this actually in the Bible?

You are allowed to revisit things you inherited.

Some beliefs may become stronger. Some may need correction.

Correction is not always deconstruction in the destructive sense.

Sometimes correction is discipleship.

Do not leave without looking at the cross.

At the center of Christianity is not a promise that good people avoid suffering.

At the center is a cross.

The Son of God suffers unjustly, voluntarily, and redemptively.

Any version of Christianity that cannot make room for suffering has already missed the center of Christianity.

And beyond the cross is resurrection.

That is the real shape of Christian hope: not escape from every wound, but a Savior who enters wounds and a resurrection that promises wounds will not win forever.

What do I do with this tonight?

Make two lists.

On one side, write what you are tempted to reject.

On the other, ask: Did Jesus actually teach this?

Take the questions one at a time. Read one Gospel—John is a good place to begin—and pay attention to Jesus Himself.

Not the version someone described to you.

The One on the page.

So here is Answer #23, stated plainly: before you walk away from God, make sure it is actually God you are rejecting. If what is collapsing is a false promise, abusive system, hypocritical example, or distorted picture of Him, let the counterfeit fall. Then look again at Jesus. You may discover the real thing is sturdier than what broke.

Questions that usually come next

Am I walking away from God—or from something that was presented as God?

That distinction matters. Some people think they are rejecting Christianity when what they are actually rejecting is hypocrisy, spiritual manipulation, legalism, abuse of authority, shallow answers, political tribalism, or rules that were treated as though they carried the same weight as Scripture.

Those experiences can distort the face of God so badly that rejecting the distortion feels like rejecting Him. Before you decide what you believe about Christ, make sure Christ Himself is the One you are evaluating.

“He that hath seen me hath seen the Father.”John 14:9 · KJV

What if Christians are the reason I want nothing to do with Christianity?

Christians can sin grievously. Churches can fail people. Leaders can misuse trust. None of that should be minimized with “people are imperfect.” Some wounds deserve repentance, accountability, protection, and time.

But a Christian’s failure is not automatically an accurate portrait of Christ. The fairest test of Christianity is not whether every Christian has resembled Jesus well. It is whether Jesus is who He claimed to be.

How do I separate Jesus from church hurt?

Start with the Gospels. Read Jesus before reading arguments about Jesus. Watch how He handles wounded people, hypocritical religion, abusive power, outsiders, doubters, sinners, and people everyone else has reduced to a label.

Then ask two different questions: “What actually happened to me?” and “What does Jesus Himself say and do?” Keeping those questions separate can expose places where someone attached God’s name to something God never asked you to carry.

Does questioning what I was taught mean I am losing my faith?

Not necessarily. Every belief should be able to survive honest examination. Some inherited convictions become stronger when tested. Others turn out to have been tradition, preference, fear, or someone else’s interpretation rather than the center of the gospel.

The goal is not deconstruction for its own sake. The goal is truth. If something false has been attached to Jesus, removing it can bring you nearer to Him rather than farther away.

Do not confuse Jesus with every person who used His name. You may need to grieve what people did, question what you were taught, and still leave the door open to discovering Christ more clearly than you saw Him before.

Where should I begin if I am not sure what I believe anymore?

Begin smaller than rebuilding your whole theology tonight. Read one Gospel. Write down the specific claims you no longer know whether you believe. Separate questions about God from wounds caused by people. Talk with someone who is safe enough not to panic at your questions.

And give Jesus the courtesy of being examined on His own terms.
