Answer 01 · God Feels Far Away

Has God really been here the whole time, or have I just told myself that to feel better?

God’s presence is not measured by how clearly you can feel Him in the moment.

About an 8 minute read · Biblical · Pastoral · A place to begin
Written by Tate Throndson · Senior pastor of Castleview Baptist Church in Castle Rock, Colorado · Pastor and author of Answers for a Broken Heart
Explore this topic: When God Feels Far Away →

The short answer

He’s always been there.

Scripture consistently shows a God who moves toward people before they know how to move toward Him. Pain can make His presence difficult to recognize, but difficulty recognizing Him is not the same thing as His absence.

If you only have 60 seconds

One truth · One Scripture · One prayer · One step
One truth

God has not abandoned you just because you cannot feel Him.

One Scripture · Psalm 34:18

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Pray this

“Lord, I cannot feel You clearly right now. Help me trust Your promise more than my perception.”

One next step

Tell God one honest sentence about where He feels absent, then read Psalm 34:18 slowly twice.

If your pain has become dangerous or you are afraid you may hurt yourself, do not carry that moment alone. Use the immediate safety pathway →

When something hurts badly enough, God can feel very far away.

You pray and hear nothing. You open the Bible and the words feel flat. Other people talk about God’s presence as though it is obvious, while you are quietly wondering whether you have simply been talking to yourself.

I do not want to answer that with a slogan.

I want to start where the Bible starts—with people hiding and God coming toward them.

The first movement after human sin is God seeking.

Genesis 3 is the story of humanity’s first rebellion.

Adam and Eve sin. Shame enters. Fear enters. They hide.

Then God speaks.

Genesis 3:9 · KJV
“And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?”

God was not asking for Adam’s coordinates.

He knew where Adam was.

The question was an invitation.

Adam is hiding from God, and God is moving toward Adam.

Long before the Bible tells you how to find God, it shows you God coming to find people first.

That pattern continues through Scripture.

God calls Abraham before Abraham builds an altar.

God sees Hagar when she feels forgotten.

God speaks to Moses in the wilderness.

God finds Elijah under a juniper tree when the prophet is exhausted and afraid.

Jesus walks toward grieving sisters, blind men, lepers, tax collectors, doubters, failures, and sinners.

The direction is remarkably consistent.

God moves toward broken people.

Why doesn’t it always feel like He is near?

Because feelings are real, but they are not omniscient.

Pain narrows vision. Anxiety changes what we notice. Grief can make an hour feel like a week and silence feel like abandonment.

None of that makes your emotions wrong. It simply means emotions cannot carry the full weight of theology.

His silence should never be mistaken for His absence.

Hebrews 13:5 · KJV
“I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

That promise does not say you will always feel held.

It says you will not be abandoned.

God describes Himself as near to broken hearts.

Psalm 34:18 · KJV
“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Near.

Not watching from a comfortable distance.

Not waiting for you to become emotionally stable before approaching.

Near to the brokenhearted.

If your heart is broken, Scripture does not describe that as the place God avoids.

It describes it as a place where He draws near.

If you cannot feel Him right now

You do not need to manufacture an experience. You do not have to force tears, warmth, certainty, or a sense of peace. Let the promise be stronger than the feeling for tonight. “I cannot feel You” and “You have promised not to leave me” can both be true sentences at the same time.

God came nearer than anyone expected.

The Bible’s greatest answer to the question of God’s distance is Jesus.

God did not merely send reassurance from heaven.

He came.

Matthew 1:23 · KJV
“They shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us.”

God with us.

In hunger. In exhaustion. At graves. Around betrayal. Under injustice. At a cross.

Whatever else Christianity says about suffering, it cannot say God remained far from it.

Presence and recognition are not the same thing.

There may be moments you only understand later.

A person who called at the right time. Strength you did not know you had. A door that closed. A Scripture that stayed with you. A friend who kept showing up.

You do not have to label every coincidence a miracle.

But you may discover, looking back, that the season you called empty contained more grace than you could recognize while you were surviving it.

What do I do with this tonight?

Do not ask yourself to feel God’s presence.

Ask yourself to respond to His promise.

Read Psalm 34 slowly. Tell Him, “I do not feel You near, but I am willing to believe You are not gone.”

Then take one small step toward connection: call someone, pray one honest sentence, sit with Scripture for five minutes, or simply stop running from the God you are afraid has already left.

So here is Answer #1, stated plainly: the Bible does not picture God hiding from broken people. It pictures broken people hiding while God comes looking. You may not recognize His presence yet. But what feels like absence may be a nearness you have not learned to see.

Questions that usually come next

If God feels far away, does that mean He actually left?

No. Scripture regularly separates what a believer feels from what God has promised. David could ask why God seemed hidden while still praying to Him. The experience of distance is real, but the feeling of absence is not proof of actual abandonment.

“I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”Hebrews 13:5 · KJV

One of the hardest things about pain is that it can shrink your field of vision. What hurts becomes loud; what is still true can become hard to feel. Faith in that moment is not pretending you feel God. It is choosing not to make your present feeling the final definition of His presence.

Why can’t I feel God when I pray or read the Bible?

Spiritual life is not always emotionally vivid. Exhaustion, grief, anxiety, disappointment, unanswered prayer, and prolonged stress can make familiar spiritual practices feel flat. That does not mean prayer has stopped being prayer or Scripture has stopped being true.

Sometimes the most faithful season is a quiet one: reading a Psalm without feeling much, praying one honest sentence, showing up to worship, or letting someone else remind you of truth when your own emotions cannot carry it.

What if God seems silent?

Silence is one of the most painful forms of waiting because it invites us to fill in the blank ourselves: He does not care. He forgot me. I did something wrong. Nothing is happening. Scripture gives us many people who lived through long stretches in which God’s activity was clearer later than it was in the moment.

If you are trying to understand what God may be doing when you cannot see it, Why can’t I see what God is doing right now? is the natural next question.

Is it wrong to tell God I feel abandoned?

No. The Psalms give you language for exactly that kind of prayer. Biblical lament is not pretending God has failed; it is bringing the experience of His apparent absence back to Him. You can say, “God, I know what You have promised, but this is what it feels like tonight.”

For tonight: do not force yourself to manufacture a spiritual feeling. Name one thing that is true even if you cannot feel it yet. Then pray one honest sentence from there.
