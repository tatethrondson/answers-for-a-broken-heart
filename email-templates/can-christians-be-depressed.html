Can Christians Be Depressed?

What does it mean when you love God, believe the Bible, and still feel like you can barely get out of bed?

Hi [First Name],

There is a particular kind of pain that comes with depression when you are a Christian. It is not just that you feel low. Sometimes you feel guilty for feeling low.

You wonder whether a Christian should feel this way. You wonder whether something is wrong with your faith. You may have prayed about it, read your Bible, gone to church, tried to be grateful, tried to think positively—and yet the heaviness is still there.

Yes. Christians can be depressed.
It does not automatically mean your faith has failed. It does not mean God has abandoned you.

You’re in good company.

One of the things I love about the Bible is that it does not sanitize the lives of its heroes. Elijah had just experienced one of the greatest spiritual victories of his life when he collapsed under a juniper tree and told God he had had enough.

“It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life.”
1 Kings 19:4 · KJV

This was Elijah—the prophet who stood against hundreds of prophets of Baal, the man who watched fire fall from heaven, the man who had seen God answer prayer in unmistakable ways. And shortly afterward, he was exhausted, afraid, isolated, and asking God to let him die.

David knew something about emotional darkness too. He talked to his own soul as though he were trying to preach truth back into a heart that could not feel what his mind knew.

“Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.”
Psalm 42:11 · KJV

Job cursed the day he was born. Jeremiah became known as the weeping prophet. Paul wrote about being troubled, perplexed, and cast down. The Bible does not hide emotional anguish.

God put those stories in Scripture because hurting people need to know that pain and faith can occupy the same heart.

Depression is not proof that God has left you.

When you are depressed, feelings can become very convincing. God must be far away. Nothing is ever going to change. I should be stronger than this.

But one of the most important distinctions we can learn in seasons of emotional pain is this:

What I feel is real. But what I feel is not always the whole truth.

David felt cast down. That feeling was real. But while David acknowledged what his soul was experiencing, he also reminded his soul of something larger: “Hope thou in God.” He did not shame himself. He did not pretend. He brought his heaviness into conversation with the truth about God.

Sometimes the most spiritual thing you can do is admit that you’re tired.

I love what happens next in Elijah’s story. God does not meet Elijah under the tree with a lecture. He lets him sleep. Then He feeds him. Then Elijah sleeps again. Only later does God begin dealing with the deeper issues in Elijah’s heart.

Sometimes we treat ourselves as though every emotional problem must have an immediate spiritual explanation. But you are not only a soul. You are also a body. You get tired. Stress accumulates. Grief takes a toll. Trauma affects us. Sleep matters. Nutrition matters. Isolation matters. Physical health matters.

Caring for your body is not the opposite of caring for your soul. Sometimes it is part of it.

Three things that might actually help this week.

01Pay attention to your body as well as your soul.Get outside. Take a walk. Eat something nourishing. Try to establish healthy sleep rhythms. If this heaviness has become persistent or is interfering with your ability to function, talk with a qualified doctor or counselor. Getting help is not a confession of weak faith. It can be an act of wisdom.
02Give God the real prayer.Not the church prayer. Not the polished prayer. The real one. God already knows what is happening inside you. God would rather hear your broken prayer than your borrowed one.
03Tell one person the truth.Depression has a way of pushing us toward isolation. You do not need to tell everybody. Tell somebody. You do not need an eloquent explanation. Sometimes three words are enough: “I’m not okay.”

One more thing I want you to know.

Psalm 42 does not end with David saying, “I finally feel better.” Instead, he speaks hope before his emotions have caught up with his theology.

Everything is not okay yet—but God is still here.

If today is a hard day, do not make yourself carry tomorrow too. Take the next step. Eat something. Go outside. Tell somebody. Pray the honest prayer. And remind your heart: “Hope thou in God.” You are not the first person who loves Jesus to walk through a dark valley. And the valley is not the whole story.

In Christ,
Tate Throndson
Pastor, Castleview Baptist Church
