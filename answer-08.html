Answer 08 · Why Did This Happen?

What do I do when the explanation never comes?

Some questions may never receive a complete answer in this life. Scripture does not pretend otherwise—but it shows us that God’s presence can become deeper than the explanation we wanted.

About a 9 minute read · Biblical · Pastoral · Honest
Written by Tate Throndson · Pastor and author of Answers for a Broken Heart

The short answer

Sometimes He gives you Himself instead of an explanation.

You may never receive a complete explanation for some of the hardest things you have lived through. Job did not. But he discovered that knowing God more deeply could steady him even without knowing why. Christianity offers more than an answer to suffering; it offers the presence of the God who entered suffering Himself.

If you only have 60 seconds

One truth · One Scripture · One prayer · One step
One truth

Sometimes the answer you receive is God's presence before it is an explanation.

One Scripture · Psalm 46:1

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”

Pray this

“God, if I cannot have the answer yet, please make Your presence enough for this moment.”

Job never received the explanation.

Job lost his children, his wealth, and his health in a collapse so severe that his friends assumed there had to be a simple explanation. They were wrong.

The reader knows there is more happening than Job can see. Job does not. And when God finally speaks, He never pulls back the curtain and gives Job the behind-the-scenes explanation readers were given at the beginning of the book.

Instead, God asks questions that remind Job how vast the difference is between divine knowledge and human sight.

Job 38:4 · KJV
“Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? declare, if thou hast understanding.”

God is not refusing relationship. He is moving Job from demanding an explanation toward encountering a Person.

Job’s great answer was God Himself.

By the end of the encounter, Job says something remarkable.

Job 42:5 · KJV
“I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear: but now mine eye seeth thee.”

Notice what he does not say: “Now I understand.” He says, in effect, “Now I know You differently.”

Sometimes God answers the question. Sometimes He answers with His presence.

Those are not always the same thing.

There are questions from my own life I still cannot explain—my mother’s multiple sclerosis, Debbie’s cancer, Caden’s injuries. Time has not handed me a neat paragraph beginning with, “Here is exactly why.” But through those years I came to know God in ways I did not know Him before them.

That does not make the suffering good. It means God can still be good there.

Christianity gives you more than a philosophy.

At some point explanations reach their limit. Even a perfectly constructed argument cannot sit beside you at 2:00 a.m. It cannot weep with you. It cannot carry you.

Christianity ultimately points somewhere more personal. It points to Jesus.

When you ask whether God understands pain, look at Christ at Lazarus’s grave. When you ask whether God cares about human suffering, look at Christ touching the people everyone else avoided. When you ask whether God has remained safely distant from death, look at the cross.

At Calvary, God did not send an explanation from heaven. He came Himself.

John 14:18 · KJV
“I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you.”

The gospel does not promise that every “why” will be answered before heaven. It promises you will not face the unanswered question alone.

The story still has an ending.

God’s presence is not a consolation prize for people who could not get the explanation. His presence comes with a promise: the suffering itself has an expiration date.

Revelation 21:4 · KJV
“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.”

That is where the Christian story is going. Not toward endless mystery and endless grief, but toward restoration in the presence of God.

Until then, we sometimes live with a sentence we cannot finish. Faith does not require us to invent the missing words. It teaches us where to lean while we wait.

If you may never know why

You can stop treating the explanation as the price of admission to peace. Tell God you still want the answer. But also ask Him to make His presence more real than your need to understand everything right now.

What do I do with this tonight?

Instead of praying only, “God, explain this,” add another prayer: “God, meet me in this.” Sit with Job 42:5. Read the Gospels slowly enough to watch how Jesus treats hurting people. Let yourself ask for comfort without demanding that comfort arrive in the form of information.

You may still want the why tomorrow. That is okay. But you do not have to wait for the why before you can have Him.

So here is Answer #8, stated plainly: sometimes God does not give the explanation we long for. Sometimes He gives us Himself instead. And in the middle of a story we still cannot understand, His presence can become the place where we learn to stand.
