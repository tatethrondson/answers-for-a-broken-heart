Suffering & Why

What do I do when the explanation never comes?

Sometimes God does not give you the explanation you want. Sometimes He gives you Himself instead.

About an 8 minute read
Written by Tate Throndson · Pastor and author of Answers for a Broken Heart
Written by Tate Throndson · Senior pastor of Castleview Baptist Church in Castle Rock, Colorado · Pastor and author of Answers for a Broken Heart
Explore this topic: Suffering & Why →

The short answer

Sometimes He gives you Himself instead of an explanation.

The Christian answer to unanswered suffering is not that every mystery will eventually become obvious. Scripture gives us something more durable: God’s presence, God’s character, and a Savior who entered suffering Himself.

Take one minute with this.

One Scripture · One prayer · One next step
One Scripture · 2 Corinthians 12:9

“My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.”

Pray this

“God, I still want to know why. Until I do—or if I never do—please be enough for me here.”

One next step

Stop trying to solve the whole story tonight. Ask only, What does faithfulness look like for the next hour?

If your pain has become dangerous or you are afraid you may hurt yourself, do not carry that moment alone. Use the immediate safety pathway →

Paul asked for the pain to leave.

Second Corinthians 12 describes something Paul calls a “thorn in the flesh.” We are not told exactly what it was, and perhaps that is intentional. It was painful enough that Paul asked God repeatedly to remove it.

2 Corinthians 12:8 · KJV
“For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me.”

Paul did not receive the answer he wanted.

Instead, he received this:

2 Corinthians 12:9 · KJV
“My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.”

God did not explain the thorn. He promised grace in the thorn.

Sometimes the answer is not an explanation. Sometimes the answer is enough grace for the next day.

Job never received the whole explanation.

Readers of Job are allowed to see conversations Job himself never hears. Job asks, argues, laments, and longs for an answer. When God finally speaks, He does not give Job a detailed account of why every loss happened.

He gives Job a larger vision of Himself.

That can feel frustrating because we want God to answer the sentence we wrote on the page. But Scripture refuses to make complete explanation the condition for trust.

The absence of an explanation does not mean the absence of God.

The cross does not explain every tragedy. It reveals God inside one.

Christianity does not answer suffering by standing outside it and offering a theory. At the center of the faith is Jesus Christ crucified.

God the Son knows betrayal, injustice, abandonment, physical agony, humiliation, grief, and death. The cross does not tell you the private reason behind every painful chapter of your life. It tells you something about the God who is with you in it.

He is not distant from suffering.

He entered it.

Can God be enough without the why?

That is not an easy question. If you are in fresh grief, it may even sound impossible.

But over time, many believers discover that faith does not survive because every question is answered. It survives because they come to know the One they are trusting.

You may still carry the question. You may ask it for years. But eventually the question may stop being the only thing in the room.

If you are exhausted from trying to make it make sense

You can stop for tonight. You are allowed to say, “I do not understand this, and I do not know if I ever will.” Then ask God for something smaller than an explanation: enough grace to sleep, enough courage to make the appointment, enough strength to get through tomorrow, enough light for the next step.

What do I do with this tonight?

Write down the explanation you wish God would give you. Then put it aside for one evening.

Read 2 Corinthians 12:9. Ask God for sufficient grace rather than complete understanding. The question can still be there tomorrow. You do not have to solve it before you rest.

So here is Answer #8, stated plainly: some explanations never come in this life. When they do not, God does not ask you to pretend the mystery is easy. He offers Himself, His grace, and enough strength for the next faithful step.

Questions that usually come next

What if God never tells me why?

Then Christianity does not require you to invent an answer. Scripture includes mysteries that remain mysteries. Job is never given the complete explanation readers receive. Paul asks for the thorn to leave and receives grace rather than removal. Some faithful people die without seeing how every piece fit together.

That means “I do not know” can be a faithful sentence.

“The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us...”Deuteronomy 29:29 · KJV

How can I trust God without an explanation?

Trust shifts from understanding the event to knowing the character of the One you are trusting. That is not blind faith. Christians look to what God has revealed—especially in Christ—while acknowledging that He has not revealed everything.

You may not know why a particular wound entered your story. You can still ask whether Jesus is trustworthy, whether the resurrection is true, whether God has been faithful before, and what Scripture actually promises now.

Is accepting mystery just a way of avoiding hard questions?

It can be, if “mystery” is used to shut down honest inquiry. But mystery is also an unavoidable part of being finite. There are questions Scripture answers and questions it does not. Intellectual humility is not intellectual surrender.

The important thing is not to use “God’s ways are higher” as a weapon against someone’s grief. The doctrine is meant to humble our confidence, not silence another person’s pain.

What do I do with the question after I stop expecting an answer?

You can keep carrying it differently. A question does not have to disappear to stop controlling every day. It can become something you place before God rather than something you solve before you are allowed to live again.

Ask different questions alongside it: “What is faithful now?” “What can still be loved?” “Who needs me?” “What help do I need?” “Where is grace today?”

You do not betray your loss by continuing to live. Moving forward is not declaring the mystery solved. It is acknowledging that an unanswered question does not have to become the boundary of your entire future.

Questions that usually come next

What if God never tells me why?

Then faith may eventually have to live without the explanation you wanted. That is not the same thing as saying the question did not matter. It means you refuse to make understanding the price God must pay before you will let Him remain God.

There are losses we may carry to the end of this life without knowing why they were permitted. Christianity does not promise that every mystery will be solved on our timetable. It promises that God can be known even while some of His ways remain beyond us.

“For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face.”1 Corinthians 13:12 · KJV

How do I stop obsessing over an answer I cannot get?

You may not be able to stop the question from returning, but you can stop giving it unlimited control over every hour. Give the question a place to go: write it, pray it, discuss it with wise people, and then return to the part of life you can actually live today.

A mystery can remain open without becoming the only open tab in your mind.

Does accepting mystery mean I am giving up intellectually?

No. Christianity has room for investigation, evidence, theology, and hard questions. Humility simply recognizes that a finite person will not possess exhaustive knowledge of an infinite God or every hidden chain of cause and consequence.

You can think seriously and still admit, “I do not know.” Those words are not intellectual surrender. Sometimes they are intellectual honesty.

What can I hold onto when explanation is gone?

Hold onto what God has made clear in Christ. He is not indifferent to suffering. He has entered it. He has borne evil rather than merely commenting on it. He has risen from death. And He has promised a future in which death, tears, and injustice do not have the last word.

When the answer never comes: you may have to stop asking the explanation to carry a weight only a Person can carry. Sometimes God does not give you the sentence you wanted. He gives you Himself to walk with through the unanswered sentence.
