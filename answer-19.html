Answer 19 · What Do I Do With This Anger?

What do I even say to God right now?

The prayer does not have to be beautiful. It just has to be real.

About an 8 minute read · Biblical · Pastoral · Practical

The short answer

Bring Him the real prayer, not the polished one.

God already knows what you feel. Prayer is not the place to hide it. Bring Him the anger, accusation, confusion, fear, disappointment, and grief as they actually are. Honest prayer keeps pain moving toward God instead of letting it harden inside you.

Sometimes the hardest part of anger is not knowing what to do with it physically.

It sits in your chest. It interrupts sleep. It makes your jaw tighten when someone says the wrong thing. It follows you into the car, the shower, the quiet house, and the minutes after everyone else has gone to bed.

And then someone says, “You should pray about it.”

But what do you pray when the actual prayer sounds nothing like the version you would say in public?

You pray the actual prayer.

God is not waiting for you to become less honest before He lets you come close.

Scripture says to pour out your heart.

Psalm 62:8 · KJV
“Trust in him at all times; ye people, pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us.”

Pouring is not careful.

It is not a teaspoon at a time.

The Psalms give us prayers that sound distressed, frightened, confused, even accusatory because prayer was never meant to be a performance for God.

It is relationship.

Borrow words when you have none.

If your own words will not come, use the Psalms.

“How long?”

“Why hast thou forgotten me?”

“Out of the depths have I cried unto thee.”

“My tears have been my meat day and night.”

Those are not polished prayers from people pretending everything is fine. They are God-given language for days when everything is not fine.

Sometimes the most spiritual prayer you can pray is Scripture’s own honest complaint.

Anger that gets prayed is still moving.

Here is why bringing anger to God matters.

Anger you pray has somewhere to go.

Anger you bury has to go somewhere too.

Sometimes it turns inward and becomes shame. You start condemning yourself for having the emotion at all.

Sometimes it settles outward and becomes bitterness. The wound begins coloring every new relationship and every new day.

Anger is fire. Bitterness is ash.

Fire is alive. It is directed. It still cares that something should be different.

Ash is what remains when the fire stops moving and settles into everything nearby.

Prayer keeps the fire moving toward the one place large enough to hold it.

What if I say something wrong to God?

You probably will.

Job did.

That is part of what makes the book of Job so reassuring. Job says things that need correction, and God corrects him.

But God does not abandon him for speaking honestly.

Correction from God is not proof you should have stayed silent. It is proof He is still in the conversation.

Think of the difference between a child who angrily tells a father what he thinks and a child who has stopped speaking to him entirely. The first relationship is strained. The second may be far more distant.

If your prayer feels ugly

Pray it anyway. You do not have to use religious vocabulary. You do not have to force yourself to end with a sentence you do not mean. Start where you actually are: “God, this hurts.” “I’m angry.” “I don’t understand.” “I wish You had stopped it.” “I need You to help me because I do not know what to do with this.” That is prayer.

A simple way to pray tonight.

If you need a place to start, try four movements—not as a formula, just as a doorway.

Tell Him what happened. Name the wound plainly.

Tell Him what you feel. Anger, fear, grief, disappointment, numbness—whatever is true.

Tell Him what you want. Justice. Healing. An answer. Sleep. Strength for tomorrow.

Tell Him what you cannot do alone. Ask Him to hold what you cannot carry and guide the next step.

You may not finish that prayer feeling better.

Prayer is not always emotional anesthesia.

Sometimes the first gift of prayer is simply that you are no longer carrying the whole conversation by yourself.

Jesus Himself prayed from agony.

In Gethsemane, Jesus did not pray a detached prayer.

Matthew 26:38-39 · KJV
“My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death… O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me…”

He named the sorrow. He named the desire. He brought both to the Father.

Honest prayer is not beneath mature faith.

Jesus prayed honestly in His hardest hour.

What do I do with this tonight?

Do not wait until you can pray nicely.

Find somewhere private. Open a Psalm if you need help. Then tell God the truth.

If you can only say one sentence, say one sentence.

If all you can do is cry, let the tears be part of the prayer.

If you need to repeat the same question ten times, repeat it.

The goal tonight is not to solve the anger. It is to stop carrying it alone.

So here is Answer #19, stated plainly: do not suppress your anger or dress it up into something more acceptable before bringing it to God. Bring Him the real prayer. The danger was never the honest prayer that reached heaven. The greater danger is the pain that never gets prayed at all.
