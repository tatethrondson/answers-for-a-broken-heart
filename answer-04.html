Answer 04 · Why Did This Happen?

If God is good, why did He make a world with so much suffering in it?

You are not wrong for feeling that something about this world is terribly wrong. Scripture says the same thing.

About an 8 minute read · Biblical · Pastoral · Honest

The short answer

This is not the world He made.

God did not look at cancer, funerals, violence, disease, drought, betrayal, and death and call them “very good.” Scripture says God made a good world, sin fractured it, and creation has been groaning ever since. Your pain is not evidence that God designed cruelty into the world. It is evidence that the world is broken—and the Christian hope is that God is not finished restoring it.

If you have ever stood beside a hospital bed, walked away from a graveside, watched someone you love suffer, or stared at a situation you could not fix, this question probably does not feel philosophical. It feels personal. If God is good—and if He could have made the world any way He wanted—why does this world hurt so much?

I do not think asking that means your faith has vanished. More often, it means your faith has been wounded. There is a difference. Wounded faith still reaches for God, even when it reaches with trembling hands. The Bible never asks hurting people to pretend the world is fine. In fact, Scripture gives language to the very thing your heart is already telling you: something here is terribly wrong.

So let’s actually answer the question. Not dodge it. Not cover it with a cliché. Answer it.

Go back to the beginning.

Before the diagnosis, before the drought, before funeral homes and hospital wings and wheelchairs, Genesis gives us a very different picture of the world. God finishes His work, looks over everything He has made, and calls it very good.

Genesis 1:31 · KJV
“And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.”

Not mostly good. Not good with a few tragic features built in. Very good. There was life without death, fellowship without shame, and creation without the groaning we recognize everywhere now. The Bible begins with a world in harmony with its Creator.

The world God created was not this one.

That matters because one of the easiest conclusions to reach in pain is that God must have designed the world to be cruel. Genesis says otherwise. Disease is not a feature of Eden. Death is not celebrated as part of the original design. Human beings were not created to stand at graves and learn how to say goodbye.

So what happened?

Genesis 3 tells the story of humanity choosing independence from God instead of trust in God. Sin enters the story, and sin never travels alone. Shame appears where there had been innocence. Fear where there had been peace. Blame where there had been harmony. The ground itself is affected. And eventually death enters a world that had been made for life.

Romans 5:12 · KJV
“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned.”

The Bible describes something larger than individual bad choices. Paul says creation itself was “made subject to vanity,” and then says that “the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now.” Creation groans. Bodies groan. Families groan. Sometimes we groan because there are no words left.

That does not mean every tragedy is a direct punishment for a particular sin. Jesus rejected that simplistic way of looking at suffering. In John 9, when the disciples tried to assign blame for a man’s blindness, Jesus would not let them reduce his pain to a neat equation. We live in a fallen world, and brokenness reaches people in ways we cannot always trace or explain.

Suffering is not evidence that God created a cruel world. It is evidence that we live in a fallen one.

Why your grief makes sense.

When someone young dies, something inside us says, This is not the way it is supposed to be. When disease slowly takes someone we love, our hearts protest. When innocent people suffer, we do not shrug and call it normal. We ache. And that ache is telling us something.

You were made for more than death. You were made for life, relationship, wholeness, and the presence of God. That is why loss feels like an intruder instead of an old friend. Scripture does not rebuke that instinct. It validates it.

You were made for a garden, and some part of you has never stopped knowing it.

If you are hurting right now

You do not have to explain away what happened in order to trust God. Christianity does not ask you to call evil good, pretend loss does not hurt, or smile at a grave. Jesus Himself wept. Faith can tell the truth about what is broken while still believing God has not abandoned the story.

God did not remain outside the broken world.

The Christian answer to suffering is not merely an explanation of how the world became broken. It is a Person who stepped into the brokenness.

Jesus knew hunger. He knew exhaustion. He knew betrayal by a friend, rejection by people He loved, injustice at the hands of authorities, physical agony, grief at a graveside, and finally death itself. God did not watch human suffering from a safe distance. In Christ, He entered it.

And the cross tells us something profound about the character of God: He is not indifferent to what sin has done to His world. He was willing to bear its cost Himself. Then the resurrection tells us something equally important: death does not get the final word.

Where hope points.

Here is the hope hidden inside the Bible’s explanation of suffering: if suffering was not God’s original design, then it will not be His final design either.

Romans 8:18 · KJV
“For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.”

Paul does not call our present suffering insignificant. He says the coming glory is so great that even real suffering will one day be seen in a larger light. Revelation takes that promise all the way to its conclusion: a day when God wipes away tears, death is gone, sorrow is gone, crying is gone, and pain is gone.

The Christian story is not that God made a cruel world and asks us to endure it. It is that God made a good world, humanity’s sin fractured it, God entered that broken world in Jesus Christ, and He is moving history toward restoration.

This chapter of the world is not the whole book.

What do I do with this tonight?

You may understand the theology and still hurt. That is normal. An answer can steady you without instantly taking away the ache. For tonight, you do not have to solve every question suffering has raised. Tell God the truth about what hurts. Read Romans 8 slowly. And when your mind begins telling you that pain is proof God has abandoned you, come back to what Scripture actually says: this brokenness is not what He called good, and it is not where He intends the story to end.

You can grieve what has been lost and still hope in what God has promised. Those two things can live in the same heart.

So here is Answer #4, stated plainly: the suffering you see in this world is not proof that God designed cruelty into His creation. It is proof that creation is fractured. God sees that fracture, entered it Himself in Jesus, and has promised that He is not done restoring what sin has broken.
