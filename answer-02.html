Answer 02 · Is He Even Here?

If God is real, why doesn’t He just show Himself?

Christianity says He has revealed Himself—and most clearly, He did it in a Person.

About an 8 minute read · Biblical · Pastoral · Christ-centered
Written by Tate Throndson · Senior pastor of Castleview Baptist Church in Castle Rock, Colorado · Pastor and author of Answers for a Broken Heart
Explore this topic: When God Feels Far Away →

The short answer

He showed you His face.

Scripture says God has left evidence of Himself in creation, spoken through His Word, and revealed His character most clearly in Jesus Christ. If you want to know what God is like, Christianity does not finally point you to an idea. It points you to Jesus.

If you only have 60 seconds

One truth · One Scripture · One prayer · One step
One truth

God has made Himself known most clearly in Jesus.

One Scripture · John 1:14

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us...”

“Jesus, if I am struggling to know what God is like, help me look at You clearly.”

The question is understandable.

If God wants people to know Him, why not make Himself unmistakable? Why not write His name across the sky? Why leave room for uncertainty?

The Bible’s answer is not that God has given no evidence.

It is that He has revealed Himself in several ways—and then went further than creation or argument ever could.

Creation speaks, even if it does not say everything.

Psalm 19:1-2 · KJV
“The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork. Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge.”

David looked at the world and saw more than beauty.

He saw communication.

Order, life, scale, complexity, beauty, consciousness, moral awareness—none of these by themselves answer every question about God. But Scripture says they are not spiritually meaningless either.

Romans 1:20 · KJV
“For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made…”

The invisible God leaves visible fingerprints.

But creation cannot tell you enough.

A mountain can make you wonder about power.

It cannot tell you whether God forgives.

A night sky can suggest vastness.

It cannot tell you whether God notices your tears.

Your own existence can raise questions about design, meaning, and purpose.

But none of those things can show you God’s heart clearly enough.

Creation can point toward a Creator. It cannot, by itself, show you His face.

God speaks in Scripture.

The Bible does not present God as mute.

Again and again, the phrase appears: the word of the Lord came.

God reveals His character, His holiness, His mercy, His purposes, and His promises through words.

But even written revelation points beyond itself.

It points to Christ.

Then the Word became flesh.

John 1:14 · KJV
“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us… full of grace and truth.”

This is Christianity’s astonishing claim.

The Creator entered creation.

The invisible God became visible.

The One beyond time entered history.

God did not merely send more information about Himself.

He came Himself.

The Author stepped into His own story.

Colossians 1:15 · KJV
“Who is the image of the invisible God…”

If you want to know how God treats grieving people, watch Jesus at Lazarus’s tomb.

If you want to know how God treats shame, watch Jesus with broken people others avoided.

If you want to know what God thinks of hypocrisy, listen to Jesus confront it.

If you want to know how far God’s love goes, look at the cross.

Jesus said seeing Him was seeing the Father.

John 14:9 · KJV
“He that hath seen me hath seen the Father.”

That does not mean Jesus is merely a visual illustration.

It means His character reveals God’s character.

This becomes especially important when circumstances tempt us to define God by what is happening.

If life feels chaotic, we assume God is careless.

If prayer seems unanswered, we assume He is indifferent.

If we hurt, we assume we are unloved.

Jesus tells us to reverse the process.

Read the circumstance through what you know of Him, not Him through whatever the circumstance is screaming tonight.

The cross settles the question of God’s heart.

The cross does not explain every tragedy.

But it does make one thing difficult to say: that God is unwilling to enter our suffering or bear cost for our rescue.

Romans 5:8 · KJV
“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

At Calvary, God’s love is not a vague idea.

It bleeds.

If God still feels hidden

Do not force yourself to feel convinced by everything at once. Start smaller. Read one Gospel and look at Jesus. Ask, “If this really is what God is like, what would that change about the way I interpret my pain?” Give yourself permission to investigate rather than pretend certainty.

What do I do with this tonight?

Open the Gospel of John.

Read one chapter.

Do not read it merely asking, What am I supposed to believe?

Ask, What kind of Person is Jesus?

Watch how He speaks. Who He moves toward. What makes Him angry. What makes Him weep. What He does at the cross.

So here is Answer #2, stated plainly: God did not leave you only clues and disappear. Creation points toward Him, Scripture speaks of Him, and Jesus reveals Him. If you want to know what God is like, look at the face Christianity says God chose to show us—the face of Christ.

Questions that usually come next

Is there evidence for God besides what I feel?

Yes. Christianity does not ask you to build the existence of God on a mood, a worship experience, or a moment when prayer felt unusually close. Scripture points outward as well as inward—to creation, to conscience, to history, and most clearly to Jesus Christ.

That matters when your emotions are exhausted. If God is real only when you feel Him, then pain gets the final vote. But Christian faith says reality is larger than your present perception.

“The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork.”Psalm 19:1 · KJV

Why doesn’t God make Himself impossible to deny?

That is a fair question. Scripture certainly portrays moments when God reveals Himself dramatically, but it does not describe faith as God overwhelming every human being with an experience so coercive that response becomes mechanical. Instead, He reveals, calls, invites, warns, and gives people real responsibility for what they do with the light they have received.

The Bible’s claim is not that God has left no evidence. It is that evidence and willingness are not always the same issue. Sometimes we need more information. Sometimes we also need to ask whether we are willing to follow the truth if it leads somewhere costly.

Has God ever actually shown us what He is like?

This is where Christianity becomes much more specific than a general argument for a Creator. Christians do not merely say, “Something must be out there.” We say God has made Himself known in Jesus.

“No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son... he hath declared him.”John 1:18 · KJV

If you want to know what Christians mean when they talk about God’s heart, look at Jesus touching lepers, receiving sinners, weeping with mourners, confronting hypocrisy, forgiving enemies, dying on a cross, and rising from the dead. Jesus is not a distraction from the question of God. He is Christianity’s central answer to it.

What if I need evidence, not reassurance?

Then investigate. Read one Gospel slowly. Ask what claims the text is actually making. Look at the resurrection question. Bring intellectual objections into the light rather than feeling guilty for having them. John explicitly says he recorded signs so that readers could examine them and believe.

“But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God...”John 20:31 · KJV
You do not have to pretend certainty: if your question is intellectual, treat it intellectually. If your question is wounded, treat the wound honestly too. God is not honored by pretending you have no questions.
