God Feels Far Away

Why can’t I see what God is doing right now?

Some of God’s work becomes clearer in hindsight, while some questions may remain unresolved in this life.

About an 8 minute read
Written by Tate Throndson · Pastor and author of Answers for a Broken Heart
Explore this topic: When God Feels Far Away →

The short answer

Some things become clearer looking back.

Most of us do not understand God’s work while we are living inside the moment. We see the loss, the closed door, the diagnosis, or the confusion. Sometimes only later do we recognize ways God was present and working that we could not see at the time. And some things may not make sense this side of heaven.

Take one minute with this.

One Scripture · One prayer · One next step
One Scripture · Proverbs 3:5

“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.”

Pray this

“God, I do not see what You are doing. Give me enough light for the step in front of me.”

Moments rarely arrive with explanations attached.

You receive the phone call without seeing what comes next.

You watch a door close without knowing whether it protected you or redirected you.

You live one day at a time while God, if He is God, sees the whole road.

That difference in perspective can make faith difficult.

We live in scenes. God sees the whole story.

Jacob discovered God had been there before he knew it.

Genesis 28 finds Jacob running.

His family relationships are fractured. His own choices have consequences. He sleeps alone with a stone for a pillow.

Nothing about the setting looks like a holy moment.

Then God gives him a dream and renews promises Jacob could not see from where he was lying.

When he wakes, Jacob says:

Genesis 28:16 · KJV
“Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not.”

God had not just arrived.

Jacob had just become aware.

The presence was already true before the recognition came.

The windshield view is limited.

When you are inside suffering, you see what is immediately in front of you.

That is not a character flaw. It is what it means to be human.

You cannot see ten years from now. You cannot see every person who will be affected. You cannot know every door a loss will close or every door it may eventually open.

And because you cannot see beyond the windshield, it is easy to assume there is nothing beyond it.

Limited sight is not proof that nothing larger is happening.

Scripture often explains the story late.

Joseph is betrayed before he understands Egypt.

Moses spends years in the wilderness before the burning bush.

Ruth loses before she knows where Bethlehem will lead.

The disciples watch Jesus die on Friday without understanding Sunday.

In each case, the people inside the story have less information than the reader looking back.

John 13:7 · KJV
“What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter.”

Jesus says this to Peter in another context, but the principle is recognizable: not now is not the same thing as never.

I have learned this pastorally too.

There have been seasons in ministry when the only thing I could see was loss, confusion, closed doors, and the feeling that momentum had disappeared.

Years later, some of those same seasons look different.

I can see provision I could not see then. Relationships God used. Opportunities that came from doors I was angry to watch close.

I would not pretend every painful part became pleasant in hindsight.

But the rearview mirror revealed things the windshield hid.

If you cannot see anything good yet

You do not have to invent a lesson. You do not have to label the tragedy a blessing. Sometimes the faithful sentence is simply: “I cannot see what You are doing yet.” The word yet leaves room for humility without requiring false optimism.

Not every explanation comes in this life.

This matters.

Some Christians talk as though every wound eventually becomes obvious.

Scripture does not promise that.

Job never receives the full behind-the-scenes explanation the reader receives.

Some graves remain graves until resurrection.

Some unanswered questions remain unanswered questions.

Christian hope is not that every mystery becomes simple before we die.

It is that mystery does not make God untrustworthy.

Remembering can strengthen present faith.

One of the repeated commands of Scripture is to remember.

Remember what God has done. Remember where He provided. Remember what He taught you before.

Psalm 77:11 · KJV
“I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old.”

When you cannot see His hand in the current chapter, borrow courage from chapters where you can see it more clearly.

What do I do with this tonight?

Make a small rearview-mirror list.

Write down three seasons you once did not understand.

Ask what you can see now that you could not see then.

Then look at the present situation and resist the sentence, God is doing nothing.

Replace it with something more humble:

I cannot see what God is doing right now.

Those are not the same claim.

So here is Answer #3, stated plainly: you may not be able to see God’s work while you are living inside the moment. Often His presence and provision become clearer only looking back, and some mysteries may wait for eternity. Do not confuse what you cannot see with what God cannot be doing.

Questions that usually come next

Why does God’s work often make more sense later?

Because we experience life one moment at a time. We know what happened today, but we do not know every consequence tomorrow will bring. Scripture repeatedly shows people interpreting events with incomplete information and only later recognizing a larger story.

Joseph could not see Genesis 50 from the pit. Ruth could not see David from the funeral. The disciples could not see Easter from Friday afternoon. Looking back does not make the painful part unreal; it simply gives us a wider field of vision.

“Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not.”Genesis 28:16 · KJV

Does trusting God mean I should pretend I understand?

No. Trust is not pretending the map makes sense. Proverbs 3:5 does not say, “Understand everything.” It says not to make your own understanding the final support for your faith. You can say, “I do not know what God is doing,” without turning that uncertainty into, “Therefore God is doing nothing.”

Faith can live with an unfinished explanation.

What if I never get the rearview-mirror moment?

Some stories become clearer with time. Others remain painful and unresolved for the rest of this life. Christianity should not promise that every tragedy will eventually become obvious to us. Scripture gives hope larger than present understanding: God sees what we do not, resurrection is real, justice is not finished, and the story extends beyond the years we can presently observe.

A gentler way to live with uncertainty: do not demand that today explain the whole story. Ask for enough light to take the next faithful step.

How do I trust God when I cannot see what He is doing?

Return to what is already clear. God’s character in Christ. The promises Scripture actually makes. The people He has given you. The next responsibility in front of you. Trust is often less dramatic than we imagine. Sometimes it looks like refusing to make a permanent conclusion about God from a chapter that is still being written.
